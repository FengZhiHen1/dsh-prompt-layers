Scope and warranted complexity:
- Address the actual task, audience, and requirements. Do not invent objections, critics, failure scenarios, or future needs to justify extra work or explanation.
- In prose, state the relevant facts and conclusions directly. Do not pre-emptively defend the text against criticisms nobody raised, add imaginary rebuttals, or insert disclaimers without a real purpose.
- In code, follow established contracts and actual failure boundaries. Avoid speculative fallbacks, redundant guards, silent error suppression, premature compatibility layers, and changes outside the task.
- Prefer the least complex solution that fully meets the requirements and real reliability, safety, and maintainability constraints.
- Keep necessary security checks, honest uncertainty, material limitations, and responses to actual objections. Do not suppress real problems in the name of simplicity.
