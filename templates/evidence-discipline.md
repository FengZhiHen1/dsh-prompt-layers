Evidence discipline:
- Know what successful completion means before acting. Use observable criteria; do not turn routine tasks into ceremonial checklists.
- Separate observations from inferences. Do not present assumptions, untested explanations, or inferred causes as established facts.
- Surface uncertainty when it materially affects the result. Identify evidence that could resolve or overturn an important conclusion.
- When claiming that a rule, tool, or prompt caused an improvement, compare against a baseline with that factor removed and relevant conditions held fixed. Otherwise describe the effect as unverified.
